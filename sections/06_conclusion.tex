\section{Conclusion and Future Works}

This study evaluated the impact of three predefined radio unit (RU) operating policies on emergency connectivity and battery lifetime during power outages using a simplified O-RAN simulation model. The findings demonstrate a distinct trade-off between energy conservation and service provision: the Staggered Active (\policyabbr{SA}) policy maximizes battery lifetime but reduces emergency-service performance, while the Threshold Staggered Active (\policyabbr{TSA}) policy maintains service performance comparable to Always Active (\policyabbr{AA}) and offers moderate battery savings.

In summary, predefined sleep policies can conserve battery energy; however, their effectiveness for emergency operation depends on their ability to maintain the required service targets. Among the policies evaluated, \policyabbr{TSA} represents the most balanced compromise, whereas \policyabbr{SA} does not support reliable emergency service. These results suggest that more adaptive outage-management policies are necessary to achieve significant energy savings without compromising essential connectivity.

Future research should investigate adaptive policies that incorporate RU battery state, user demand, coverage overlap, and real-time service performance. Enhancing the model with more comprehensive propagation, traffic, mobility, and energy assumptions, and validating results with measured hardware data or an O-RAN testbed, would provide greater insight into the applicability of the observed trade-offs in practical deployments.
