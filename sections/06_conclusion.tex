\section{Conclusion}

This study evaluated how three predefined RU operating policies affect emergency connectivity and battery lifetime during a power outage in a simplified O-RAN simulation model. The results answer the first research question by demonstrating a clear energy--service trade-off. Always Active (AA) provided the strongest emergency-service performance, with a median SLA-based network lifetime of 21.0 simulation steps. Staggered Active (SA) achieved the greatest battery benefit: its median mean RU depletion time was 46.2 steps, compared with 31.2 steps for AA. However, this approximately 48\% increase came with the weakest service performance, including a median SLA-based network lifetime of 0.0 steps. Thus, periodic sleeping conserves battery energy but can remove essential coverage or capacity before the emergency-service target is reliably sustained.

The findings also address the second research question regarding the suitability of these policies for emergency operation. The Threshold Staggered Active (TSA) policy represented the most effective compromise among those evaluated. TSA maintained a median SLA-based network lifetime comparable to AA (20.5 versus 21.0 steps) and increased the median mean RU depletion time to 33.4 steps, approximately 7\% higher than the AA baseline. However, this improvement remains modest, and the greater energy savings observed with SA are unreliable under the specified SLA. Consequently, while the evaluated fixed policies serve as useful comparative baselines, they do not constitute a sufficiently robust general solution for emergency outage management. Energy-saving strategies should therefore consider their immediate impact on the configured service target, rather than focusing solely on battery preservation.

Future research should investigate adaptive policies that incorporate RU battery state, user demand, coverage overlap, and real-time service performance. Enhancing the model with more comprehensive propagation, traffic, mobility, and energy assumptions, and validating results with measured hardware data or an O-RAN testbed, would provide greater insight into the applicability of the observed trade-offs in practical deployments.
