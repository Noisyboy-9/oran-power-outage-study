\begin{abstract}
This study assesses the impact of predefined O-RAN Radio Unit (RU) sleep policies on emergency connectivity and battery lifetime during power outages. A custom discrete-time simulator compares an Always Active (AA) baseline with Staggered Active (SA) and Threshold Staggered Active (TSA) policies. The evaluation utilizes ten paired repetitions and measures served-user fraction, horizon-average emergency Quality of Service (QoS), RU battery depletion time, and Service Level Agreement (SLA)-based network lifetime at a served-user threshold of 0.7. The AA policy establishes the strongest emergency-service baseline, with a median network lifetime of 21.0 simulation steps. The SA policy extends the median RU depletion time from 31.2 to 46.2 steps, an increase of about 48\%, but yields a median SLA-based network lifetime of 0.0 steps. The TSA policy maintains a network lifetime similar to AA, at 20.5 steps, while increasing mean RU depletion time to 33.4 steps. These results demonstrate that battery savings alone do not ensure reliable emergency operation. In the modeled configuration, TSA is identified as the most suitable evaluated sleep policy for maintaining the service target, although its battery benefit is modest and underscores the need for more adaptive outage-management strategies.
\end{abstract}
