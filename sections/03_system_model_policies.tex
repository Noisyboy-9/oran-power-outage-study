\section{System Model and Evaluated Policies}

This section defines the discrete-time outage model implemented by the
simulator.  The policies below are fixed policies evaluated comparatively;
they are not an optimisation method.

\subsection{Network and Link-Quality Model}

The outage starts before the first observation and lasts for a horizon of
\(K\) unit-duration timesteps.  Let \(U\) and \(R\) denote the sets of users
and RUs, respectively.  Users and RUs are stationary and are placed uniformly
without replacement on the cells of a discrete \(W\times H\) grid.  Thus, no
two entities occupy the same cell.  With locations \(p_u=(x_u,y_u)\) and
\(p_r=(x_r,y_r)\), their Euclidean distance is
\begin{equation}
 d_{u,r}=\sqrt{(x_u-x_r)^2+(y_u-y_r)^2}.
\end{equation}
An in-range bipartite edge exists only when \(d_{u,r}<\rho\), where \(\rho\)
is the coverage radius.  This in-range topology is fixed during a run.

At every observation timestamp \(t\), each in-range edge receives a newly
sampled synthetic link weight
\begin{equation}
 w_{u,r}(t)=\left(1-\frac{d_{u,r}}{\rho}\right)\xi_{u,r}(t),
 \qquad \xi_{u,r}(t)\in(0,1].
\end{equation}
The inclusive threshold \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) determines whether an in-range
edge is a candidate for service: \(w_{u,r}(t)\geq\lambda\).  These weights
are an abstract link-quality signal only; the simulator does not calculate
SINR, interference, propagation, throughput, or traffic demand.

\subsection{Association and Outage Assumptions}

Let \(a_{u,r}(t)\in\{0,1\}\) be the association decision.  Associations are
exclusive, so \(\sum_{r\in R}a_{u,r}(t)\leq1\): a user has one serving RU or
none.  The load on RU \(r\) is
\begin{equation}
 n_r(t)=\sum_{u\in U}a_{u,r}(t), \qquad n_r(t)\leq C_r,
\end{equation}
where \(C_r\) is its user capacity.  For each user in ascending user-ID
order, the simulator considers candidates with \(w_{u,r}(t)\geq\lambda\),
ordered by descending weight and then ascending RU ID.  It selects the first
RU that is active, non-depleted, and not full; otherwise the user remains
unassociated.  Hence service is defined by association rather than merely by
an in-range edge:
\begin{equation}
 y_u(t)=\sum_{r\in R}a_{u,r}(t), \qquad
 \mathrm{QoS}(t)=\frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{u\in U}y_u(t).
\end{equation}

The outage affects the RUs, whose initial battery energies are \(b_r^0\).
There is no recharge, recovery, or state-switching time or energy cost.  An
RU is depleted when its battery is zero and cannot serve users.

\subsection{Battery Model and State Transitions}

Let \(b_r(t)\) be the battery after the update at timestamp \(t\), and let
\(x_r(t)\in\{\mathrm{S},\mathrm{A}\}\) be its observed sleep or active state.
With positive consumption rates \(P_s\), \(P_0\), \(P_1\), and \(P_m\), the
consumption implied by the preceding state and association is
\begin{equation}
 c_r(t-1)=
 \begin{cases}
 P_s, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{S},\\
 P_0, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)=0,\\
 P_1, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)=1,\\
 n_r(t-1)P_m, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For a unit-duration timestep, battery discharge is clamped at zero:
\begin{equation}
 b_r(t)=\max\{0,b_r(t-1)-c_r(t-1)\}.
\end{equation}
The state \(x_r(0)\) is the configured initial state, and initial associations
are observed at \(t=0\).  For each \(t=1,\ldots,K\), the simulator charges
according to the preceding status
and associations, applies the controller, regenerates link weights, rebuilds
associations, and then observes metrics.  A controller can activate a
selected RU only when \(b_r(t)\geq P_0\); otherwise it is sleep.

\subsection{Evaluated Policies}

All policies use the same placement, link-weight, association, and battery
models.  At each controller timestamp \(t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\),
\emph{always active} selects every RU, subject to the activation condition
above.  At a controller timestamp \(t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), \emph{periodic
staggered sleep} selects RU \(r\) exactly when
\begin{equation}
 r\bmod2=\left\lfloor\frac{t}{10}\right\rfloor\bmod2,
\end{equation}
so even IDs are selected when \(\lfloor t/10\rfloor\bmod2=0\), and odd IDs
are selected otherwise; the other parity sleeps.  The 10-timestep schedule
uses the global timestamp.

For \emph{threshold-triggered staggered sleep}, define the trigger timestamp
\(\tau\), when it exists, as the first controller timestamp at which every RU
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \tau=\min\left\{t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:b_r(t)\leq
 \frac{\theta}{100}b_r^0\ \text{for all}\ r\in R\right\}.
\end{equation}
All RUs are selected at controller timestamps before \(\tau\).  At every
\(t\in\{\tau,\ldots,K\}\), including \(t=\tau\), the periodic staggered rule
applies permanently, using the same global timestamp rather than restarting
its phase.  If no such \(\tau\) is observed, all RUs remain selected at every
controller timestamp.

\subsection{Observed Metrics}

The average emergency QoS includes all \(K+1\) observations:
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\mathrm{QoS}}=\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{t=0}^{K}\mathrm{QoS}(t).
\end{equation}
The observed depletion time of RU \(r\) is
\begin{equation}
 D_r=\begin{cases}
 \min\{t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}:b_r(t)=0\}, & \text{if observed},\\
 \infty, & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For an emergency-service target \(\eta\), observed network lifetime is
\begin{equation}
 L_\eta=\begin{cases}
 0, & \mathrm{QoS}(0)<\eta,\\
 t^\star-1, & t^\star=\min\{t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:\mathrm{QoS}(t)<\eta\},\\
 \infty, & \mathrm{QoS}(t)\geq\eta\ \text{for all}\ t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus, finite metrics are observation-bound, while \(\infty\) denotes that no
depletion or service violation was observed by the end of the horizon.
