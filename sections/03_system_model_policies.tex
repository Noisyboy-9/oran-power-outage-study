\section{System Model and Evaluated Policies}
\label{sec:system-model}
\subsection{Network and Link-Quality Model}

The outage starts before the first time step and lasts for a horizon of
\(K\) unit-duration time steps.  Let \(U\) and \(R\) denote the sets of users
and Radio Units (RUs), respectively.  Users and RUs are stationary, and their locations are
sampled uniformly at random without replacement from the cells of a discrete
\(W\times H\) grid.  Consequently, no two entities occupy the same cell.  With
locations \(p_u=(x_u,y_u)\) and
\(p_r=(x_r,y_r)\), their Euclidean distance is
\begin{equation}
 d_{u,r}=\sqrt{(x_u-x_r)^2+(y_u-y_r)^2}.
\end{equation}
An in-range bipartite edge exists only when \(d_{u,r}<\rho\), where \(\rho\)
is the coverage radius.  This in-range topology is fixed during a run.

At every observation timestamp \(t\), each in-range edge receives a newly
sampled synthetic link weight:
\begin{equation}
 w_{u,r}(t)=\left(1-\frac{d_{u,r}}{\rho}\right)\xi_{u,r}(t),
 \qquad \xi_{u,r}(t)\in(0,1].
 \label{eq:link-quality}
\end{equation}
The normalized score \(w_{u,r}(t)\) is the simulator's proxy for real-world
SINR behavior, not a calculated physical SINR value.  Its deterministic
factor \((1-d_{u,r}/\rho)\) represents expected link deterioration with
distance: nearby pairs have greater baseline quality, which approaches zero
as the coverage boundary is approached.  Independently at each observation,
\(\xi_{u,r}(t)\) is sampled uniformly at random from \((0,1]\).  This factor
represents temporal variation that can make real SINR fluctuate even for
stationary users, such as changing channel or interference conditions,
without explicitly computing those mechanisms.  The inclusive threshold
\(\lambda\in[0,1]\) gives the eligibility rule in plain terms: an in-range
link with \(w_{u,r}(t)\geq\lambda\) will be considered for association, whereas
a link below \(\lambda\) will not.  

\subsection{Association and Outage Assumptions}

Let \(a_{u,r}(t)\in\{0,1\}\) be the association decision.  Associations are
exclusive, so \(\sum_{r\in R}a_{u,r}(t)\leq1\): a user has one serving RU or
none.  The load on RU \(r\) is
\begin{equation}
 n_r(t)=\sum_{u\in U}a_{u,r}(t), \qquad n_r(t)\leq C_r,
 \label{eq:ru-load}
\end{equation}
where \(C_r\) is its user capacity. 

For each user in ascending user-ID order, the simulator considers candidates with \(w_{u,r}(t)\geq\lambda\),
ordered by descending weight and then ascending RU ID.  It scans this ranked
list and associates the user with the first RU that is active, has positive
battery energy, and has spare capacity.  If no candidate meets all three
conditions, the user remains unassociated.  The complete association map is recomputed independently at
every observation timestamp; associations are neither sticky nor persistent
across time steps.  The simulator models no handover hysteresis or minimum
residence time.  Therefore, a user served by one RU at \(t-1\) may be assigned
to another at \(t\) when that RU ranks first among the newly ordered eligible
candidates. Such re-association incurs no modeled delay, temporary service interruption, or
additional energy cost.  

Service is therefore defined by association rather
than merely by an in-range edge:
\begin{equation}
 y_u(t)=\sum_{r\in R}a_{u,r}(t).
\end{equation}

The outage affects the RUs, whose initial battery energies are \(b_r^0\).
There is no recharge, recovery, or state-switching time or energy cost.  An
RU is depleted when its battery is zero and cannot serve users.

\subsection{Battery Model and State Transitions}

Let \(b_r(t)\) be the battery after the update at timestamp \(t\), and let
\(x_r(t)\in\{\mathrm{S},\mathrm{A}\}\) be its observed sleep or active state.
With positive consumption rates \(P_s\), \(P_0\), \(P_1\), and \(P_m\), the
consumption implied by the preceding state and association is
\begin{equation}
 c_r(t-1)=
 \begin{cases}
 P_s, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{S},\\
 P_0, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)=0,\\
 P_1, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)=1,\\
 n_r(t-1)P_m, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)\geq2.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:ru-consumption}
\end{equation}
For a unit-duration time step, battery discharge is clamped at zero:
\begin{equation}
 b_r(t)=\max\{0,b_r(t-1)-c_r(t-1)\}.
 \label{eq:battery-update}
\end{equation}
The state \(x_r(0)\) is the configured initial state, and initial associations
are observed at \(t=0\).  

For each \(t=1,\ldots,K\), the simulator reduces the battery capacities
according to the preceding status and associations, applies the policy decisions, 
regenerates link weights, rebuilds associations, and then observes metrics.  
A controller can activate a selected RU only when \(b_r(t)\geq P_0\); otherwise it is sleep.

\subsection{Evaluated Policies}

The simulator implements and compares three deterministic policies:
\emph{AlwaysActive}, \emph{StaggeredActive}, and
\emph{ThresholdStaggeredActive}.  \emph{AlwaysActive} keeps all feasible RUs
active, \emph{StaggeredActive} alternates fixed RU groups, and
\emph{ThresholdStaggeredActive} begins with all RUs selected before switching
to the same staggered schedule after a battery-based trigger.

All policies use the same placement, link-weight, association, and battery
models.  At each controller timestamp \(t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), a policy
selects the RUs intended to be active; the activation condition specified
above still prevents a selected RU with insufficient battery energy from
becoming active.

The baseline \emph{AlwaysActive} policy selects every RU at every controller
timestamp.

The \emph{StaggeredActive} policy selects RU \(r\) exactly when
\begin{equation}
 r\bmod2=\left\lfloor\frac{t}{10}\right\rfloor\bmod2,
 \label{eq:staggered-policy}
\end{equation}
at controller timestamp \(t\).  Thus, even-ID RUs are selected when
\(\lfloor t/10\rfloor\bmod2=0\), whereas odd-ID RUs are selected otherwise;
the other group sleeps.  The policy uses the global timestamp to alternate
between the two groups in consecutive 10-timestamp intervals.

The \emph{ThresholdStaggeredActive} policy follows \emph{AlwaysActive} until
the battery trigger occurs.  When it exists, the trigger timestamp \(\tau\)
is the first controller timestamp at which every RU satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \tau=\min\left\{t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:b_r(t)\leq
 \frac{\theta}{100}b_r^0\ \text{for all}\ r\in R\right\}.
 \label{eq:threshold-trigger}
\end{equation}
At every \(t\in\{\tau,\ldots,K\}\), including \(t=\tau\), it applies the
\emph{StaggeredActive} rule using the same global timestamp, rather than
restarting the schedule.  If no such \(\tau\) occurs, the policy selects all
RUs at every controller timestamp.

\subsection{Observed Metrics}

emergency QoS is defined as the proportion of users receiving service at
timestamp \(t\):
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{QoS}(t)=\frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{u\in U}y_u(t).
\end{equation}
It ranges from zero, when no user is associated, to one, when all users are
associated.  The average emergency QoS over the simulation horizon includes
all \(K+1\) observations:
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\mathrm{QoS}}=\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{t=0}^{K}\mathrm{QoS}(t).
\end{equation}

The observed battery-depletion time of RU \(r\) is
\begin{equation}
 D_r=\begin{cases}
 \min\{t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}:b_r(t)=0\}, & \text{if observed},\\
 \infty, & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For an emergency-service target \(\eta\), the observed network lifetime is
\begin{equation}
 L_\eta=\begin{cases}
 0, & \mathrm{QoS}(0)<\eta,\\
 t^\star-1, & t^\star=\min\{t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:\mathrm{QoS}(t)<\eta\},\\
 \infty, & \mathrm{QoS}(t)\geq\eta\ \text{for all}\ t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus, \(D_r\) and \(L_\eta\) are bounded by the observation horizon; a value
of \(\infty\) means that the corresponding depletion or service-target
violation was not observed by the end of the simulation.
