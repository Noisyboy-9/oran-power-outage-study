\section{System Model and Evaluated Policies}
\label{sec:system-model}

This section defines the network-wide outage model used to evaluate the predefined RU operating policies. It first specifies the network, link-quality, and association assumptions, then describes the RU battery model, state transitions, evaluated policies, and emergency-service metrics.

\subsection{Network and Link-Quality Model}

We consider a mobile network operator (MNO) that provides cellular coverage over a discrete \(W\times H\) grid through \(N\) O-RUs (hereafter RUs), represented by the set \(R\), where \(|R|=N\). The coverage area contains a set \(U\) of users. A network-wide grid-power outage begins before the first time step, during which the RUs draw on finite local batteries. The O-CU and O-DU are assumed to remain available through backup power and are therefore excluded from the energy model. This abstraction isolates the RU-level trade-off between energy use and emergency service during the outage.

The outage lasts for a horizon of \(K\) unit-duration time steps. Users and RUs are stationary, and their locations are sampled uniformly at random without replacement from the cells of the grid. Consequently, no two entities occupy the same cell. For each user \(u\in U\) and RU \(r\in R\), with locations \(p_u=(x_u,y_u)\) and \(p_r=(x_r,y_r)\), let \(d_{u,r}\) denote their Euclidean distance. An in-range edge exists between \(u\) and \(r\) only when the user lies within the RU's coverage radius, i.e., \(d_{u,r}<\rho\), where \(\rho\) denotes the coverage radius.

At every observation timestamp \(t\), each in-range edge is assigned a newly sampled synthetic link weight, which serves as a proxy for real-world SINR behavior rather than a calculated physical SINR value:
\begin{equation}
 w_{u,r}(t)=\left(1-\frac{d_{u,r}}{\rho}\right)\xi_{u,r}(t),
 \qquad \xi_{u,r}(t)\in(0,1].
 \label{eq:link-quality}
\end{equation}
The deterministic factor \((1-\frac{d_{u,r}}{\rho})\) represents expected link deterioration with distance: nearby pairs have greater baseline quality, which approaches zero as the coverage boundary is approached.  Independently at each observation, \(\xi_{u,r}(t)\) is sampled uniformly at random from \((0,1]\).  This factor represents temporal variation that can make real SINR fluctuate even for stationary users, such as changing channel or interference conditions, without explicitly computing those mechanisms.  The inclusive threshold \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) gives the eligibility rule in plain terms: an in-range link with \(w_{u,r}(t)\geq\lambda\) will be considered for association, whereas a link below \(\lambda\) will not.

\subsection{Association and Outage Assumptions}

Let \(a_{u,r}(t)\in\{0,1\}\) denote whether user \(u\) is associated with RU \(r\) at time \(t\). Associations are exclusive, so \(\sum_{r\in R}a_{u,r}(t)\leq1\): a user has one serving RU or none. The number of users served by RU \(r\) at timestamp \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
 n_r(t)=\sum_{u\in U}a_{u,r}(t), \qquad n_r(t)\leq C_r,
 \label{eq:ru-load}
\end{equation}
where \(C_r\) is the maximum number of users that RU \(r\) can serve simultaneously.

At each observation timestamp \(t\), users are considered in ascending user-ID order. For each user \(u\), the candidate RUs are those for which \(w_{u,r}(t)\geq\lambda\). The candidates are ranked by descending link weight and then by ascending RU ID. The user is associated with the highest-ranked candidate that is active, has positive battery energy, and has not reached its maximum simultaneous-user limit. If no candidate meets these conditions, the user remains unassociated. Associations are recomputed independently at every observation timestamp; the model includes no handover hysteresis or minimum residence time. Therefore, a user served by one RU at \(t-1\) may be assigned to another at \(t\) when that RU ranks first among the newly ranked candidates. Such re-association incurs no modeled delay, temporary service interruption, or additional energy cost.

Service is therefore defined by association rather than merely by an in-range edge:
\begin{equation}
 y_u(t)=\sum_{r\in R}a_{u,r}(t).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Battery Model and State Transitions}
The outage affects the RUs, whose initial battery energies are \(b_r^0\). We assume that there is no recharge, recovery, or state-switching time or energy cost.  An RU is depleted when its battery is zero and cannot serve users.

Let \(b_r(t)\) be the battery after the update at timestamp \(t\), and let \(x_r(t)\in\{\mathrm{S},\mathrm{A}\}\) be its observed sleep or active state. At each unit-duration time step, the energy consumption of RU \(r\) depends on its state and its number of served users at the preceding timestamp. A sleeping RU consumes \(P_s\). An active RU consumes \(P_0\) when serving no users and \(P_1\) when serving one user; when serving two or more users, it consumes \(P_m\) per user. Accordingly, the energy consumed by RU \(r\) is
\begin{equation}
 c_r(t-1)=
 \begin{cases}
 P_s, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{S},\\
 P_0, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)=0,\\
 P_1, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)=1,\\
 n_r(t-1)P_m, & x_r(t-1)=\mathrm{A},\ n_r(t-1)\geq2.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:ru-consumption}
\end{equation}
For a unit-duration time step, battery discharge is clamped at zero:
\begin{equation}
 b_r(t)=\max\{0,b_r(t-1)-c_r(t-1)\}.
 \label{eq:battery-update}
\end{equation}
The state \(x_r(0)\) is the configured initial state, and initial associations are observed at \(t=0\).

For each \(t=1,\ldots,K\), the simulator reduces the battery capacities according to the preceding status and associations, applies the policy decisions,  regenerates link weights, rebuilds associations, and then observes metrics.   Also, a controller can activate a selected RU only when \(b_r(t)\geq P_0\); otherwise it remains in sleep state.

\subsection{Evaluated Policies}

The simulator implements and compares three deterministic policies: \emph{Always Active}, \emph{Staggered Active}, and \emph{Threshold Staggered Active}.  \emph{Always Active} keeps all feasible RUs active, \emph{Staggered Active} alternates fixed RU groups, and \emph{Threshold Staggered Active} begins with all RUs selected before switching to the same staggered schedule after a battery-based trigger.

All policies use the same placement, link-weight, association, and battery models.  At each controller timestamp \(t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}\), a policy selects the RUs intended to be active; the activation condition specified above still prevents a selected RU with insufficient battery energy from becoming active.

The baseline \emph{Always Active} policy selects every RU at every controller timestamp.

The \emph{Staggered Active} policy selects RU \(r\) exactly when
\begin{equation}
 r\bmod2=\left\lfloor\frac{t}{10}\right\rfloor\bmod2,
 \label{eq:staggered-policy}
\end{equation}
at controller timestamp \(t\).  Thus, even-ID RUs are selected when \(\lfloor t/10\rfloor\bmod2=0\), whereas odd-ID RUs are selected otherwise; the other group sleeps.  The policy uses the global timestamp to alternate between the two groups in consecutive 10-timestamp intervals.

The \emph{Threshold Staggered Active} policy follows \emph{Always Active} until the battery trigger occurs.  When it exists, the trigger timestamp \(\tau\) is the first controller timestamp at which every RU satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \tau=\min\left\{t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:b_r(t)\leq
 \frac{\theta}{100}b_r^0\ \text{for all}\ r\in R\right\}.
 \label{eq:threshold-trigger}
\end{equation}
At every \(t\in\{\tau,\ldots,K\}\), including \(t=\tau\), it applies the \emph{Staggered Active} rule using the same global timestamp, rather than restarting the schedule.  If no such \(\tau\) occurs, the policy selects all RUs at every controller timestamp.

\subsection{Observed Metrics}

emergency QoS is defined as the proportion of users receiving service at timestamp \(t\):
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{QoS}(t)=\frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{u\in U}y_u(t).
\end{equation}
It ranges from zero, when no user is associated, to one, when all users are associated.  The average emergency QoS over the simulation horizon includes all \(K+1\) observations:
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\mathrm{QoS}}=\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{t=0}^{K}\mathrm{QoS}(t).
\end{equation}

The observed battery-depletion time of RU \(r\) is
\begin{equation}
 D_r=\begin{cases}
 \min\{t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}:b_r(t)=0\}, & \text{if observed},\\
 \infty, & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation} For an emergency-service target \(\eta\), the observed network lifetime is
\begin{equation}
 L_\eta=\begin{cases}
 0, & \mathrm{QoS}(0)<\eta,\\
 t^\star-1, & t^\star=\min\{t\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:\mathrm{QoS}(t)<\eta\},\\
 \infty, & \mathrm{QoS}(t)\geq\eta\ \text{for all}\ t\in\{0,\ldots,K\}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation} Thus, \(D_r\) and \(L_\eta\) are bounded by the observation horizon; a value of \(\infty\) means that the corresponding depletion or service-target violation was not observed by the end of the simulation.
